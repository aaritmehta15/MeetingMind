AI Copilot & Code Intelligence Engineering Roadmap Sync
Date: November 14, 2025 | Duration: 40 mins
Participants: Dr. Aris Thorne (Head of AI & ML Engineering), Maya Lin (Staff Frontend Engineer), Carlos Mendoza (Principal Infrastructure Lead), Zoe Kravitz (VP of Product Management)

Zoe Kravitz: Good morning team. Let's kick off our sprint planning and Q1 architecture roadmap sync for the MeetingMind AI Copilot and real-time streaming engine. We have three crucial items to lock down: first, our latency benchmarks on token streaming via Server-Sent Events; second, our model serving infrastructure migration from vLLM to TensorRT-LLM on Nvidia H100s; and third, the customer-facing SLA commitments for our enterprise beta launch. Aris, let's start with the latency telemetry from last week's load test.

Dr. Aris Thorne: Thanks, Zoe. In our staging cluster tests across 10,000 simulated concurrent developer streams, our Time-To-First-Token (TTFT) averaged 185 milliseconds on the 70B parameter model. However, during burst traffic periods where prompt length exceeded 8,000 tokens—such as when ingesting entire quarterly meeting transcripts—TTFT degraded to 840 milliseconds. The main bottleneck was KV-cache memory allocation contention in our serving containers.

Carlos Mendoza: Right. To solve that memory thrashing, we ran an extensive comparative benchmark between vLLM with PagedAttention and TensorRT-LLM using FP8 quantization on our new 8x H100 HGX cluster. The results were dramatic: TensorRT-LLM delivered a 2.4x increase in decoding throughput and reduced P99 latency from 840 milliseconds down to 210 milliseconds under maximum load. Furthermore, memory consumption per active session dropped by 45%, allowing us to host up to 40 concurrent conversation contexts per GPU node without OOM failures.

Zoe Kravitz: That is a game changer for our enterprise tier. What are the cost implications and infrastructure readiness?

Carlos Mendoza: Migrating to TensorRT-LLM requires converting our model weights into the proprietary TensorRT engine format. We have automated this conversion pipeline in Triton Inference Server. The total infrastructure cost will be $28,500 per month, which represents a net savings of $14,200 compared to our previous multi-region vLLM deployment. I will finalize the Kubernetes Helm chart deployment on our production EKS cluster by Wednesday, November 19th at 3:00 PM.

Zoe Kravitz: Fantastic. Let's confirm that decision: we officially approve the migration to TensorRT-LLM on Triton Inference Server with FP8 quantization for our production copilot endpoints. Maya, what is the frontend state regarding the streaming UI and syntax highlighting?

Maya Lin: On the frontend, we migrated our REST polling hooks to a native WebSocket and Server-Sent Events architecture. The new streaming component supports smooth Markdown rendering, interactive citation highlights, and Monaco code editor integration. However, we discovered a rendering regression in Safari when handling simultaneous streaming chunks with large JSON payload structures.

Dr. Aris Thorne: Is that related to Safari's WebKit text layout throttling on high-frequency DOM mutations?

Maya Lin: Exactly. To resolve this, I implemented a 30-millisecond requestAnimationFrame throttling buffer that aggregates token deltas before triggering React state updates. I tested it across Chrome 124, Firefox 126, and Safari 17.4, and CPU utilization on client laptops dropped from 68% down to 12%. I will push the PR with end-to-end Cypress test coverage by Friday, November 21st at 12:00 PM.

Zoe Kravitz: Outstanding work, Maya. Aris, what about our hallucination benchmarks and Grounded Citation Guard accuracy on multi-turn dialogue?

Dr. Aris Thorne: We evaluated the Citation Guard against our 500-sample gold standard benchmark suite. Exact verbatim citation matching achieved 99.4% precision with zero false positives. For the 0.6% edge cases involving multi-line quote breaks, our newly added Unicode quote and whitespace normalization successfully recovered every grounded claim. I will document the complete benchmark evaluation paper and publish the internal technical whitepaper by Monday, November 24th at 5:00 PM.

Zoe Kravitz: Excellent. Let's summarize our action items and wrap up. Carlos owns the Triton Helm charts by Nov 19th, Maya delivers the Safari streaming PR by Nov 21st, and Aris submits the technical evaluation whitepaper by Nov 24th. Thank you everyone.
